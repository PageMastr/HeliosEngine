# Round-5 remaining (minor) review gaps

## [minor] (backend) plan/05-backend-services.md
**Gap:** There is no design or acceptance criterion for detecting cheats and bots on the server. 04 §9 sends 'aim statistics' to Trust, but Trust's Phase 4 rules in 05 §1.16 cover only wealth jumps, item-count drift and RMT graphs. Nothing detects aimbots or triggerbots in Destiny-class PvP, input automation, or the farming and mining bots that threaten EVE- and SWG-class economies. AAA-SEC-3 measures only speed and teleport hacks. The anti-cheat vendor decision is deferred to Phase 4 (08 §1.14), although R05 records that Bungie eventually needed kernel anti-cheat, and a native Linux client limits which vendors can be used.

**Proposed fix:** Add a §1.16a Trust detection framework with three parts. (1) Feature extraction in cells from data that already exists: snap angle and time to target from the lag-compensation history, hit rate against target visibility and occlusion, input-timing entropy, route and loop periodicity, session length. (2) A labelled corpus from red-team cheat bots (seam-fighter variants with aim hacks, and farm bots). (3) Delayed ban-wave tooling tied to the §1.17 cases. Add AAA-SEC-9 (Ph4): on the red-team corpus, ≥ 90 % of aimbot and farm-bot sessions are flagged within 24 h, with ≤ 0.1 % false positives on NS-4.1's honest bot population. Move the vendor decision (EAC or BattlEye, including Linux-client support) to the Phase 3 security WP (WP-3.11).

## [minor] (backend) plan/05-backend-services.md
**Gap:** §3.6's shard-primary capacity model, and A5's '±20 %' WAL and IOPS assertion, count only the ledger, the fence and world scripts. §3.1 puts much more on the same primary: account progression at 2k flushes/s with rows up to 32 KiB (each BYTEA update rewrites the row), leaderboards at ≥ 10k writes/s (§1.12), market order writes at 2k/s (A7), durable timers with 1 M pending, activity snapshots, ratings, fleet rosters, world flags at ≤ 500/s, mail and structure upkeep. Together these could roughly double the stated 27.6 MB/s of WAL. That matters because §3.6 itself names the synchronous standby's single-process replay as the binding limit. A5 passes on a partial mix.

**Proposed fix:** Add a row per writer to §3.6 (rows/s, WAL/s and IOPS at the expected and design rates), and put the combined mix into `helios-loadgen` for A5 (Ph4). If the total exceeds replay capacity, move leaderboards, activity and account progression to their own cluster, like the persistence cluster, or store progression as append-only deltas with periodic compaction instead of whole-row rewrites. Update §3.1's store table to match.

## [minor] (backend) plan/04-networking-and-servers.md
**Gap:** §10.2's replay contract misses decisions that depend on load. §6.2a chooses the co-location host from measured `ag_tick_us`, and refuses on tick p95 > 70 %, overload stage ≥ 2, or the 5 %/10 % tick-budget caps. §6.3's handoff NACK also fires on overload. Both change which cell simulates what, and whether a contact is co-simulated or handled as a puppet, yet neither is a logged replay event. §10.2's last row even says overload stage 'never' changes simulation state. NS-3.8 replays only NS-2.4's single-cell recordings, so no criterion replays a multi-cell zone through handoffs, co-location, migration holds or `ZoneRejoin`, which is exactly where seam bugs live.

**Proposed fix:** Add replay rows `ColocDecision{tick, pair, host | nack, reason, measured inputs}` and `HandoffDecision{tick, ag, accept | nack, reason}` to §10.2, and correct the overload row. Add an NS-3.8 clause: every cell's log from NS-3.12(c) (the seam furball) and from NS-3.11(c) (a multi-cell migration with an expired hold) replays bit-exact, one cell at a time, with the chained `MigratedFrom` headers verified.

## [minor] (backend) plan/05-backend-services.md
**Gap:** Replicants are kept off their cells' host and rack (§1.4.6), but not off their availability zone, since a zone has 3 racks at Phase 4. Yet §6.7's DR table says cells lost with an availability zone are 'restored from replicants in world zones from Phase 4'. When a replicant shares its cells' zone, the zone loss takes both, and recovery falls back to checkpoints: up to 30 s of state lost, not ≤ 1 s. A19 does not measure state loss in its zone-loss drill, and the cross-zone trunk cost of AZ-disjoint replicants is not modelled.

**Proposed fix:** Choose one and make it consistent. Either (a) place each replicant in a different availability zone from all its cells, add the cross-zone stream cost (≈ 20 Mbit/s per cell, ≈ 4 Gbit/s at Phase 4) to §6.4, and have A19 assert per-AG rollback ≤ 1 s for the lost zone's world cells. Or (b) correct the §6.7 table to 'checkpoints, ≤ 30 s' for a zone loss and state that bound in A19.

## [minor] (backend) plan/05-backend-services.md
**Gap:** §6.5's NATS least-privilege rules contradict subjects the plan uses elsewhere. Cells may publish only to `rpc.<shard>.{orch,ledger,persist,fence,character,activity,chat,world}.>`, but they call world scripts on `rpc.<shard>.ws.<script>.<partition>.<Method>` (§1.23). They read only DIRECTORY, CONFIG, PERMS, ACTIVITY and WORLD, but they watch KV `GROUP` for fleet rosters (§1.12.1, §2.4). They subscribe only to `ctl.<shard>.cell.<own-id>.>`, but 04 §6.6 sends `ctl.<shard>.cell.all.gateway_dead`. No permissions are given for the world-script host, the replicant, the gateway or the voice roles. Credentials are also per role, not per process, so the cell ID in the `Helios-Fence` header is never authenticated: a compromised cell could write fenced requests as another cell.

**Proposed fix:** Replace the prose with a generated permission matrix per role (cell, world-script host, replicant, gateway, voice, and each Go service), derived from the schemac service and subject declarations, with a CI check that every subject used in the code is granted. Issue a NATS credential per process (an nkey or JWT whose claim carries the process ID). Have the ledger, persistence and world-script services check that the fence header's cell equals the authenticated process ID.

## [minor] (backend) plan/05-backend-services.md
**Gap:** No workflow covers maintenance of SERVER hosts, the Kubernetes nodes that run cells. `Drain(process, maintenance)` exists, and gateway boxes have a full roll protocol (§6.3.1). But nothing connects node cordoning to the orchestrator's `Drain` for every cell, replicant and standby on that node. Nothing sets Agones' eviction policy (allocated game servers block `kubectl drain`). And no rule caps concurrent node maintenance against the warm pool sized for one rack. Monthly kernel patching of about 30 hosts therefore either stalls or takes the crash path. A14 tests only binary rolls.

**Proposed fix:** Add §6.1a, a node-maintenance controller. Cordoning a node triggers orchestrator `Drain(maintenance)` for every process on it. GameServers run with Agones `eviction.safe: Never`, and the node is released only once it is empty. At most one rack's worth of nodes is in maintenance at a time, and never while `WarmPoolBelowDomain` is open. Add an A14 clause (Ph4): a host-patch roll of every SERVER node under 50k bots, with a hitch p99 ≤ 1 s for each region moved and 0 disconnects.

## [minor] (engine) plan/02-engine-runtime.md
**Gap:** Bit-exact physics prediction and replay are asserted, but only for queries. 02 §5.4 says 'a hull-versus-static contact predicted by the client is bit-identical to the cell's'. RT-19/RT-20, 06 §8.1a/GP-4a/GP-4d and 04 NS-2.4/NS-3.8 rely on the same claim, but the plan removes BodyID dependence only from cast tie-breaks (02 line ~1741, 06 line 1248). Jolt's contact solver order still depends on BodyID. In the vendored tree, ContactConstraintManager.cpp builds each constraint's mSortKey from a hash of SubShapeIDPair{body1 ID, subshape1, body2 ID, subshape2}, and SortContacts orders by that key. CharacterVirtual's ContactOrderingPredicate orders contacts by mBodyB first. The predictor's PhysicsSystem assigns different BodyIDs from the cell's, and 06 line 1248 says so itself. The cell's own IDs also depend on the add/remove history of tile bodies. The Gauss-Seidel order for a hull, character or vehicle touching several tiles therefore differs, and the results diverge in the low bits. The rollback snapshot (chassis plus VehicleConstraint SaveState) also omits the warm-start contact cache, so a re-simulated tick starts from different accumulated impulses than the cell's.

**Proposed fix:** Add a 'Jolt ordering independence' rule to 02 §7.1, with one of two options. (a) A small vendored Jolt patch (tracked in third_party/PATCHES): derive the contact sort key and CharacterVirtual's contact ordering from a stable 64-bit key stored in body user data (EntityId, or TileKey plus sub-shape), not from BodyID. (b) A deterministic BodyID policy: create bodies with BodyInterface::CreateBodyWithID, using IDs derived from stable keys through a shared deterministic allocator used on both cell and predictor. Also include the owned body's contact-cache state (SaveState with EStateRecoverType::Contacts, filtered to the owned body and its touching tiles) in the 06 §8.1a rollback snapshot, or clear the warm start identically on both sides at every rollback. Extend RT-03/RT-19 with a variant that permutes BodyID allocation order on one side and must still produce identical state hashes. Mirror the rule in 04 §10.2 and 06 §8.2.

## [minor] (engine) plan/04-networking-and-servers.md
**Gap:** The replay model is underspecified for long-lived world zones. 04 §10.2 says 'a zone replays bit-exactly from its initial checkpoint plus a log', with a 30 min ring on local NVMe. The plan never defines a mid-session keyframe that holds everything bit-exact replay needs. Missing pieces: Jolt body and contact state including BodyID assignment, the TerrainFence and tile-body state, RNG streams, and pending sim-inbox items. 04 §6.7 also says a Luau thread cannot be serialized, so any zone with a live coroutine cannot restart from a keyframe. Taken together, the 30-minute ring cannot be replayed on its own for a zone that has been up for hours, and NS-2.4's 10-minute replay only proves the start-of-instance case.

**Proposed fix:** Define a 'replay keyframe' in §10.2 by listing its contents: ECS snapshot, per-grid Jolt SaveState including contacts, the BodyID or stable-key map, tile-body set and fence state, RNG streams, the inbox queue and script state. Keyframes are cut only at script-quiescent points where no coroutine is suspended, or long waits are restricted to serializable timers and state machines. Alternatively, limit bit-exact replay to spans that begin at instance start, planned migration or recovery checkpoints, and say so. Add an NS clause that replays a 10-minute window beginning at a keyframe taken at least 1 h into a soak.

## [minor] (engine) plan/02-engine-runtime.md
**Gap:** The gate spec lags the code in two places and the vendored config in one. (1) 02 §1.1 describes the illegal-instruction backstop as showing the gate message and exiting 78. It says nothing about deliberate traps (ud2/ud1/ud0 from __debugbreak, __builtin_trap or assert paths), and it does not say the handler must pass control to, or be replaced by, the crash handler once it installs. As written, a real crash could produce the CPU-unsupported message with no minidump, which undermines the stability gates. The working-tree hooks already add hcg_is_deliberate_trap passthrough (Win32 VEH and POSIX SA_SIGINFO), so this is reverse drift. (2) 02 §1.1 (line ~153) says 'tp_mimalloc pins MI_WIN_INIT_USE_CRT_TLS=1', but third_party/CMakeLists.txt defines only MI_STATIC_LIB on tp_mimalloc. The claim is currently false, and no WP owns making it true. (3) 09 §5.10.4(b)'s CONF row still says 'The objects keep /GS (the allowlist admits __security_check_cookie)'. The working tree's helios_cpu_gate_sources() now applies /GS- and -fno-stack-protector, and isa_allowlist.cmake drops the cookie symbols, so the row is stale.

**Proposed fix:** In 02 §1.1: the backstop decodes the faulting opcode, passes ud2 (0F 0B), ud1 (0F B9) and ud0 (0F FF) straight through, and handles only genuine ISA faults (#UD on AVX/AVX2/FMA/BMI encodings). It is removed, or chained behind the crash handler, as soon as the crash handler installs; audit check 5 verifies this. Add 'define MI_WIN_INIT_USE_CRT_TLS=1 on tp_mimalloc and assert it in the audit' to WP-0.2r, or reword 02 to 'relies on mimalloc's default mode' plus a test that asserts no _pRawDllMain. Refresh the 09 §5.10.4(b) row to match the working tree and add the trap-passthrough behaviour to the CONF table.
